Academic Integrity & Governance

Competition Rules & Rubric

Official framework governing participant enrollment, pathway integrity, unexpected disruption adaptation, and rubric scoring.

1. Eligibility & Participant Limits

Institutional

1.1 Verified Email: Only students possessing an active @egspec.edu institutional email address are eligible to register as Members.

1.2 Participant Quota: Each engineering competition is strictly capped at a default maximum of 100 participants. Once all 100 slots are filled via atomic transactional ledger enrollment, no additional participants may register.

1.3 Single Enrollment: A student may only enroll once in any specific competition.

2. Problem-Solving Pathways & Integrity

Equal Evaluation

Pathway A (AI-Assisted): Participants utilizing LLMs, AI agents, or code assistance engines must provide transparent declarations regarding tools used, prompts submitted, human modifications, and empirical verification methodologies.

Pathway B (Without AI): Participants working purely with native logic and classical algorithms must demonstrate first-principles engineering reasoning and mathematical proofs.

Equal Evaluation: Both pathways are scored under the exact same seven weighted criteria. Neither pathway enjoys an inherent score bonus.

3. Standardized Seven-Criteria Weighted Rubric

Total = 100%
  • 1. Problem Understanding (15%): Insight into real-world industrial constraints and core challenges.
  • 2. Technical Depth (15%): Quality of architectural design, logic flow, and algorithm complexity.
  • 3. Adaptability to Problem Shift (20% — Primary Tie-Breaker): Resilience, speed, and elegance in responding to Version 2.0 operational disruptions.
  • 4. Code Completeness (15%): Working code, automated tests, repository structure, and documentation.
  • 5. Practical Feasibility (15%): Hardware resource constraints, edge deployability, and cost factors.
  • 6. Pathway Integrity (10%): Transparency in AI declarations or pure engineering algorithmic proofs.
  • 7. Innovation (10%): Novelty of engineering approach and out-of-the-box resilience strategies.

4. Operational Disruption & Version Immutability

Disruption Policy

Immutability of Version 1.0: Version 1.0 specifications are locked into the ledger and can never be rewritten or modified.

Version 2.0 Shift: Organizers drop unpredictable technical challenges (e.g. sensor outages, communication dropouts, power restrictions). Participants must submit an adaptive revision.

Tamper-Proof Receipts: Both initial (NEX-SUB-...) and revision (NEX-REV-...) submissions generate cryptographic SHA-256 receipts confirming ownership and timestamps.

5. Leaderboard Confidentiality & Appeals

Confidentiality

Confidential Evaluation: To safeguard competition integrity, leaderboards remain concealed from participants until official publication by organizers.

Private Judge Notes: Internal judge moderation notes remain permanently confidential and are never exposed to participants.

Tie-Breaker Hierarchy: Ties in total score are automatically broken by the score in Criterion 3 (Adaptability to Problem Shift).
